Recall the definitions from \cref{intro:sec:basic} regarding set theory, functions, automata, and formal languages. 
Here, we introduce additional notions, facts, and results not covered previously.
\Cref{sec:logic_set_theory_number_theory} gives more basic notions
from logic, set and number theory.
Further notions related to functions, in particular for functions on finite sets,
are introduced in \cref{prelim:sec:fun}.
\Cref{pelim:sec:trans} introduces transformations and gives some results
for transformations on finite sets.
In \cref{prelim:sec:relations} we introduce relations.
Semigroups, monoids, groups, semirings, matrices, and transformation semigroups
are introduced in \cref{prelim:sec:algebra}. Order-theoretical notions are introduced in \cref{prelim:sec:order}.
\Cref{sec:formal_language_theory} gives more background on formal language theory
and introduces regular expressions.
Graphs and connectedness notions are introduced in \cref{prelim:sec:graphs}. 
\Cref{prelim:sec:complexity} introduces computational complexity theory
and our theoretical framework to measure the resource requirements of algorithms.
\Cref{prelim:sec:remarks-reduction,prelim:sec:remarks_func_comp} 
discusses conventions and notational choices.
Lastly, \cref{prelim:prelim:discussion} 
gives an overview of the literature related to the introduced notions.

The present chapter serves to fix notation. We assume some familiarity and prior exposure
with general concepts from logic, set theory, number theory, basic algebra, formal language theory, and complexity theory.
