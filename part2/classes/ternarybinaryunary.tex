The following tight bound on shortest words of rank $r$ in unary automata
is probably folklore. For completeness, we give a short proof.

\begin{proposition}
	\label{prelim:prop:unary_bound}
	Let $\semiautomat{A}{Q}{\{a\}}{\delta}$
	be an $n$-state unary automaton.
	If there exists a word of rank $r$ in $\mathcal A$,
	then there exists a word of length at most $n - r$
	with rank equal to~$r$,	and this bound is tight
	even in unary automata where~$r$ is the minimal rank among all words. 
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof} 
	Suppose we have $r = |\delta(Q, a^i)|$
	for some $i \ge 0$.
	Then, by \cref{prelim:word_in_PA},
	there exists  $0 \le j \le  n - r$
	such that $r = |\delta(Q, a^j)|$.
	Tightness for $1 \le r < n$ is shown
	by $\semiautomat{A_n}{\{q_1,\ldots,q_n\}}{\{a\}}{\delta}$
	with $\applytrans{\delta}{q_i}{a} = q_{\min\{i+1,n-r\}}$
	for $i \in \{1,\ldots,n-r\}$ and $\applytrans{\delta}{q_i}{a} = q_i$
	for $i \in \{n-r+1,\ldots,n\}$ (see \cref{prelim:fig:unary_tight}).    
    Alternatively, the automata family $\semiautomat{B_{n}}{\{1\ldots,n\}}{\{a\}}{\delta}$
	with $\delta(i, a) = \min\{i+1,n\}$
	has $a^{n-r}$ as the shortest word of rank at most~$r$
	with $\delta(\{1,\ldots,n\}, a^{n-r}) = \{n - r + 1, n - r + 2, \ldots, n\}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark} \label{prelim:classes:rem:unary-seq-aut}
	The unary automata $\semiautomat{C_{n}}{\{1\ldots,n\}}{\{a\}}{\delta}$
	with $\delta(i, a) = \min\{i+1,n\}$
	would also show tightness of the bound from \cref{prelim:prop:unary_bound} for general unary automata
    since the shortest word of rank at most~$r$
	in $\mathcal C_{n}$ is $a^{n - r}$
	with $\delta(\{1,\ldots,n\}, a^{n-r}) = \{n - r + 1, n - r + 2, \ldots, n\}$.
	Here $r$ is not the minimal rank among all words
	for $r > 1$.		
\end{remark}
