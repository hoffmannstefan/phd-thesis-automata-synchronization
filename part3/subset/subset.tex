In this chapter, we investigate the subset mapping properties in automata and transformation semigroups, motivated by minimality conditions for automata accepting sets of synchronizing words. The results presented here can be combined to derive lower bounds for the size of these automata.

In \cref{sync_max:sec:power_automat_for_SynA},
we introduce and motivate the notions of reachability
and distinguishability. 
We do this by first observing
that the power automaton accepts the set of synchronizing,
and then naturally obtain both notions
via minimality conditions for accepting automata from \cref{prelim:subsubsec:min_aut} applied to the power automaton. 
Thus, these notions derive from necessary conditions
characterizing the size of minimal automata for sets of synchronizing words.

\Cref{sync_max:sec:reachable} investigates the reachability of subsets of states in automata.
We derive synchronizability conditions and 
structural limitations in terms of the reachability
of families of subsets of states.

\Cref{sync_max:sec:distinguish}
is concerned with distinguishability,
where we investigate
its relation to synchronizability and 
consider different families of pairwise distinguishable subsets
and their relations. We also introduce the novel notion of completely distinguishable automaton.

Reachability and distinguishability transfer readily from automata
to transformation semigroups. \Cref{sync_max:sec:from_aut_to_semigroups}
shortly discusses this and some differences that arise as a transformation
semigroup might not contain the identity transformation.
